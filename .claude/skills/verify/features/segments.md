# Segments

A segment is a saved rule over the people or accounts of a client, judged per identity across every list (#938). A published segment keeps its members: it adds the people who match and removes the people who stop matching, and it writes only what changed. `segment_transitions` records each change; an `enter` record is the entry event that automations read (#1029). The web pages are `/segments` (list), `/segments/new` (editor), `/segments/<id>` (editor of a saved segment) and `/segments/<id>/members`, over `/v1/segments`.

## Sub-features

- `segments-preview` counts the identities that match a rule now, with the rows that have no identity yet, before anything is saved.
- `segments-save` creates a draft, renames it or changes its rule, and refuses an edit of a version that is no longer current (409 `SEGMENT.STALE_VERSION`).
- `segments-publish` makes the matching identities members, silently (`baseline`) or as entries with `include_current: true`, and a refresh that finds no change writes nothing.
- `segments-entries` records each join (`enter` or `baseline`), exit and merge in `segment_transitions`, with the next episode after an exit, and hands the `enter` records out in `seq` order through `app.segments.entries.entries_after`.
- `segments-updates` keeps a published segment current after a data change: a write marks the people it touches (`segment_dirty`), and the beat `evaluate_segments` judges them again within about a minute.
- `segments-pause` is the admin setting `segments_evaluation_paused`: the beat waits and keeps every mark, and preview, publish, refresh and a rule edit of a published segment answer 503 `SEGMENT.EVALUATION_PAUSED`. `why` still answers.
- `segments-members` pages the live members, each with its representative row.
- `segments-why` explains how the rule judges one identity, condition by condition, with the rows of every list.
- `segments-excluded` leaves out excluded rows: a person who matches only through an excluded row is in no segment.
- `segments-rule-errors` refuses a bad rule with 422 `SEGMENT.INVALID_RULE` and the JSON pointer of the node.
- `segments-scope` returns 404 `SEGMENT.NOT_FOUND` for a segment of another client or organization.

## How to get to it (user POV)

Any HTTP client with an `sbk_` key ([api-keys](api-keys.md)) or a session token: `curl -H "Authorization: Bearer <token>" "$VERIFY_API/v1/segments"`. In the app, **Segmenti** in the sidebar, under **Automazioni**, opens `/segments`.

## Driving it with bin/browser

Preconditions:

- `bin/stack up` with `VERIFY_ID` set. No vendor key and no LLM key are needed: `VERIFY_LLM=off` is fine. A segment reads cells only.
- A user, `bin/stack seed-user segments@verify.local verify-pass-1234`, and a session token for that user. Without the browser, sign in with `curl -c jar.txt -H 'content-type: application/json' -H "Origin: $VERIFY_WEB" -d '{"email":"segments@verify.local","password":"verify-pass-1234"}' "$VERIFY_WEB/api/auth/sign-in/email"`, then read `token` from `curl -b jar.txt "$VERIFY_WEB/api/auth/token"` (`T` below). Keep `jar.txt` out of the artifacts.
- One API call as that user (`GET /v1/projects`), which creates the user's first client. Then seed: `bin/stack psql -v email=segments@verify.local -f - < features/segments-seed.sql`. It adds `Segmenti A` and `Segmenti B` with Marco Bianchi in both (one identity through his LinkedIn URL), Giulia Verdi, Luca Neri (a CTO in `Segmenti B`, and a CEO with an email and fit 95 on an excluded row of `Segmenti A`) and `Senza alias` (no alias, no identity), and links the rows. The output prints `linked 5` and the ids of the two lists.
- The rule `R` below is "title contains `ceo`, email filled, fit at least 80": `{"type":"group","op":"and","children":[{"type":"text","field":{"kind":"canonical","key":"title"},"op":"contains","values":["ceo"]},{"type":"presence","field":{"kind":"canonical","key":"email"},"op":"is_filled"},{"type":"fit","op":"gte","low":80}]}`.

- **Preview.** `POST /v1/segments/preview {"entity":"person","rule":R}` answers 200 with `count` 2 (Marco and Giulia), `pending` 0 and `unidentified_rows` 1. Marco matches although no single row of his has a title, an email and the fit: his title and fit are in `Segmenti A`, his email in `Segmenti B`.
- **Save.** `POST /v1/segments {"name":"CEO con email","entity":"person","rule":R}` answers 201 with `version` 1, `published_at` `null` and `members` 0. `POST /v1/segments/<id>/refresh` answers 409 `SEGMENT.NOT_PUBLISHED`.
- **Publish.** `POST /v1/segments/<id>/publish` answers `applied: {joined: 2, entered: 0, exited: 0, merged: 0, members: 2}` and `max_credits: 0`. `segment_transitions` holds two `baseline` records. A second segment published with `{"include_current":true}` answers `entered: 2` and holds two `enter` records, `seq` ascending. Before and after, `SELECT (SELECT count(*) FROM vendor_calls), (SELECT count(*) FROM credit_transactions), (SELECT sum(credits_remaining) FROM user_credits)` gives the same row. `SELECT identity_id, xmin, ctid FROM segment_members WHERE segment_id = '<id>'` gives two rows; two `refresh` calls answer `joined: 0, exited: 0`, the same query gives the same `xmin` and `ctid`, and `segment_transitions` gains no record.
- **Members.** `GET /v1/segments/<id>/members?limit=1` gives one member and a `next_cursor`. Pass it as `after`: the second page gives the other member and `next_cursor: null`. Marco's row is the one of `Segmenti A` (fit 85, the best of his rows).
- **Why.** `GET /v1/segments/<id>/members/<Marco's identity>/why` answers `matches: true, member: true`. Under `rule.children`, the title condition holds the `Segmenti A` row (`hit: true`, `CEO`), the email condition the `Segmenti B` row and the fit condition both rows (85 hit, 60 not). Luca's identity answers `matches: false, member: false` with `CTO` not a hit.
- **Excluded row.** Luca matches `R` only through his excluded `Segmenti A` row, so the preview `count` stays 2 and his `why` lists no `Segmenti A` row. Run `UPDATE list_rows SET excluded = false WHERE list_id = '<list_a>' AND cells->>'full_name' = 'Luca Neri'`, and the preview answers `count` 3. Set `excluded = true` again before the next step.
- **Entries.** Make Luca a CEO with fit 88 by SQL (`UPDATE list_rows SET cells = cells || '{"title": "CEO"}', relevance_score = 88 WHERE ...`), then `refresh` twice: the first answers `entered: 1`, the second `joined: 0`, and Luca has one `enter` record. Set Marco's `Segmenti A` fit to 60 and `refresh`: an `exit` record at episode 1. Set it back to 85 and `refresh`: an `enter` record at episode 2. Read them with `bin/stack compose exec -T api python -`, calling `entries_after(conn, '<id>', 0, 2)` on a `psycopg.connect(settings.database_url, row_factory=dict_row)` connection: each entry has `seq, segment_id, project_id, identity_id, entity, episode, list_row_id, segment_version, entered_at`, and the call from the last `seq` returns the rest.
- **Edit.** `PATCH /v1/segments/<id> {"version":1,"rule":<R with "low":88>}` answers `segment.version` 2, `segment.members` 1 and `leaving: 1`: Marco exits (`segment_members.exited_at` set, an `exit` record) and Giulia stays. Widening the rule again answers `joining: 1` and records Marco as `baseline`; with `"include_new":true` he is an `enter`. The same `PATCH` with `"version":1` again answers 409 `SEGMENT.STALE_VERSION` with `version: 2`. Before each edit, `POST /v1/segments/preview` with the new rule and `"segment_id":"<id>"` answers the same `joining`, `leaving` and `max_credits` as the `PATCH` that follows, and `segment_transitions` gains no record. Set Marco's fit to 60 and `refresh`, set it back to 85 with no refresh, then `PATCH` the same rule with no `include_new`: `joining` is 0 and Marco's new record is `enter` at the episode after his last `exit`, because the data caused that join.
- **Updates.** With the segment published, change Giulia's title by hand: `PATCH /v1/lists/<Segmenti A>/rows/<Giulia's row>` with `{"cells":{"title":"CTO"}}`. `segment_dirty` holds one mark for her row, and within 60 s (two beats) `segment_transitions` holds an `exit` for her; set `CEO` back and an `enter` at the next episode follows. `bin/stack logs beat worker | grep segments.drain` shows one `segments.drain.batch` line for each batch, with `ms`. Soft-delete `Segmenti B` (`DELETE /v1/lists/<id>`): Marco loses his email and exits, Giulia stays. Add an email to `Senza alias` by hand: the link beat links the row, and the next segment beat writes one `enter` if the row now matches.
- **No segment, no marks.** In a client with no published segment, `UPDATE list_rows SET cells = cells || '{"note": "x"}'` over all its rows leaves `SELECT count(*) FROM segment_dirty` at 0.
- **Many marks.** An update of more than 5,000 rows of one client leaves one `segments.drain.fallback` line, then one `segments.drain.full` line for each published segment, and no mark. The members then equal a fresh `refresh`, which answers `joined: 0, exited: 0`.
- **Catch-up.** A publish sets `segments.full_due_at` 20 minutes later. A write that left no mark (for example with the trigger off: `ALTER TABLE list_rows DISABLE TRIGGER segment_mark_rows`, the write, then `ENABLE`) joins after `UPDATE segments SET full_due_at = now()`, at the next beat, as one `enter`.
- **Pause.** `PUT /v1/admin/settings {"key":"segments_evaluation_paused","value":true}` as an admin (or `UPDATE settings SET value = 'true' WHERE key = 'segments_evaluation_paused'`). Wait 30 s for every process to read it. Edit a cell: the mark stays for two beats and nothing changes; `POST /v1/segments/<id>/publish` answers 503 `SEGMENT.EVALUATION_PAUSED`. Set it back to `false`: the edit is judged at the next beat.
- **Bad rule.** A rule four groups deep answers 422 `SEGMENT.INVALID_RULE` with `path` `/children/0/children/0/children/0` and `reason` `too_deep`.
- **Ledger.** In `BEGIN ... ROLLBACK`, a second `enter` for an existing `(segment_id, identity_id, episode)` fails on `segment_transitions_one_join_per_episode`. As `zenbree_app` (`SET LOCAL ROLE zenbree_app; SELECT zb.enter_org('<org>')`), `UPDATE` and `DELETE` on `segment_transitions` fail with `permission denied`, and another organization reads 0 records.
- **Scope.** Seed a second user and take its token. `GET /v1/segments/<id>`, `/members`, `/members/<Marco>/why` and `POST /refresh` answer 404 `SEGMENT.NOT_FOUND`, and its `GET /v1/segments` is empty.
- **Proof.** Save the JSON answers and the psql output under `$VERIFY_ARTIFACTS/segments/`, with tokens redacted.

## Gotchas

- A seeded user has no client until its first API call: the seed then fails on `lists.project_id` being null.
- A row written by SQL is not marked for the link beat. The seed links its rows with `audience_link_row`. A row with no identity is in no segment, but `unidentified_rows` counts it.
- A cell under a held per-row charge defers its identity (`pending`): it neither joins nor exits until the charge settles. The beat keeps a mark for that person and judges them again 60 s later. The seed has none; `api/tests/test_segments_sql.py` and `api/tests/test_segments_drain.py` cover it.
- The beat runs as the organization, never as the segment's creator, so a creator removed from the organization does not stop updates.
- The pause is read through a 30 s cache in each process: a change takes up to 30 s to reach the api and the worker.
- A rule that names a list or column deleted later freezes the segment: `problems` lists it and an evaluation writes nothing. `why` returns the same `problems`. When the beat skips a frozen segment, it owes the segment a full evaluation 5 minutes later, and again until one runs. A restore of the list or column then brings the members up to date within 5 minutes.
- `enter_org` takes the organization id as a value. Reading it from `segments` as `zenbree_app` before the call returns no row, so the scope is never entered.
- Without `TEST_DATABASE_URL`, pytest skips every `test_*_sql.py` test and every test that uses the database: 18 of the 23 segments tests skipped in one gate run. Run `eval "$(./harness/testdb.sh)"` from the repo root before `cd api && uv run pytest -rs tests/test_segments_*.py`, and check that `-rs` reports no skip.
