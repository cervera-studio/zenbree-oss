# Decisioni di prodotto e architettura

**Prodotto:** Zenbree trasforma un brief in chat in liste di lead, contatti verificati e campagne email e LinkedIn. Nel codice, `outreach` resta un termine di dominio.

**Stack:**

- `web/`: Next.js, Better Auth con JWT e admin, Tailwind, Pragmatic Drag and Drop, Motion e Zustand per lo stato condiviso.
- `api/`: FastAPI, verifica JWT tramite JWKS e chiavi personali `sbk_`.
- Worker e scheduler: Celery con Redis; il timeout di visibilità deve superare il job più lungo.
- PostgreSQL 17 con SQL esplicito, migrazioni versionate e isolamento delle organizzazioni.
- LLM: DeepSeek primario e Anthropic come fallback, con Cloudflare AI Gateway opzionale.
- Le integrazioni si attivano con le credenziali della propria installazione. Ogni installazione gestisce hosting, backup e osservabilità.

**Campagne:** `outreach_campaigns`, `outreach_campaign_targets` e `outreach_campaign_sends` separano la campagna, i destinatari e gli invii. Gli step ordinati sono JSONB e sono validati da `app/outreach/domain/campaign.py`.

Una campagna nasce in bozza e richiede attivazione esplicita. Approvazione, claim persistente prima della chiamata al provider, stop-on-reply, esclusioni e controllo duplicati proteggono ogni invio. Un esito ignoto non autorizza un reinvio automatico. Sender pool, finestre di invio, cap, pacing, isolamento e kill switch restano obbligatori. LinkedIn passa da Unipile: Zenbree conserva l'account id, mai password o cookie.

**Billing — handoff decisions (#175, 2026-10-02).** Each one takes the recommended option; the owner can still pick the alternative, which is not implemented.
- **Receipt:** Stripe sends it. Every payment already produces a Stripe invoice with Stripe Tax (`automatic_tax`, `tax_id_collection`, `invoice_creation` for packs in `app/billing/routes.py`), so there is no E5 receipt email of our own. Alternative left out: our own receipt email, with Stripe's turned off. FatturaPA/SDI stays with #111.
- **VAT:** displayed prices exclude VAT (B2B). The billing page shows them as «€29 + IVA» / «€29/mese + IVA», and the terms page says the same. Stripe Tax computes VAT at checkout: Italian buyers pay 22% (€29 + 22% = €35,38), while EU businesses with a valid VAT number get reverse charge (`_checkout` in `app/billing/routes.py`). Alternative left out: VAT-inclusive prices.
- **Welcome credits:** 25 (50 until the 2026-10-07 repricing), no card required. The Better Auth signup hook (`web/lib/signup-credits.ts`) opens the wallet with the admin setting `signup_bonus_credits`, and with 25 (also the `user_credits.credits_remaining` default, set by `api/db/20261007143736_credit_repricing.sql`) when the setting is missing or not a whole number from 0 to 2147483647 (#368). The landing names these 25 welcome credits (hero and closing section, #355): changing the setting means changing that copy too. Alternative left out: 100 credits.
- **Plan credits expiry:** this is what the code does already (`expire_plan_credits` in `app/billing/plan_period.py`). Each paid period opens with the plan's allowance; plan credits are spent before purchased ones, and whatever is left of the allowance expires (`plan_expiry`) at renewal or when the subscription ends. Top-ups and packs never expire. Alternative left out: plan credits that roll over.

**Landing page — location (#177, 2026-10-03; replaced by the 2026-10-06 and 2026-10-15 entries below).** The landing is the existing public route in `web/` (`app/(public)/welcome`): `proxy.ts` rewrites signed-out visitors at `/` on zenbree.com to `/welcome`, signed-in ones keep the app. Prices come from the public `GET /v1/billing/plans` and `/packs`, the same source as the billing page. Alternative left out: a separate marketing site with its own domain routing.

**Landing and app hosts (2026-10-06; its `/welcome` part replaced by the 2026-10-15 entry below).** `zenbree.com` and `www.zenbree.com` serve the Astro landing (`landing/`); the app moves to `app.zenbree.com` (`ops/cloudflared.yml`). Any path the landing does not have answers 308 to the same path on the app host, so old links (auth emails, OAuth callbacks, `/u/<token>`, bookmarks) keep working. This replaces the #177 location: the web's `/welcome` stays for signed-out visitors at the app's `/`. Alternatives left out: the landing on a subdomain with the app on the main domain, and the web proxying the landing for signed-out visitors.

**One marketing page (2026-10-15).** The app's signed-out `/` redirects (307) to the Astro landing, and `/welcome` is gone: `/welcome` and `/welcome/...` redirect there too, signed in or out. The design port did not cover `/welcome`, and it repeated the landing, so the product had two marketing pages that drifted apart. The landing is now the homepage that Google's OAuth verification sees. `NEXT_PUBLIC_LANDING_URL` (a web build arg; CI defaults to `https://zenbree.com`, compose to `http://localhost:4321`) names the landing for `proxy.ts` and for the help item of the account menu, which opens the landing's FAQ. Italian visitors go to the landing's `/`, the other languages to `/<locale>/`. The landing's FAQ section has the id `faq` in every language, and its `Content` type holds it there, so the help item and old `/welcome#faq` links open the FAQ. When the value is empty, not a URL, or the app's own host, nothing redirects to it: `/` shows sign-in to a signed-out visitor, `/welcome` goes to the app's `/`, and the help item is hidden. Alternative left out: port `/welcome` to the design and keep both pages.


**Credit repricing (owner, 2026-10-07).** 1 credit = 1 verified email, about €0.05, like Apollo's credits, instead of ~€0.015 with 1 credit per row. Rows (searches) are free; an email found costs 1, a phone number found 8 (Apollo bills 8 of its credits per phone, measured on prod), an AI cell 1. Plans and packs keep their Stripe amounts with fewer credits: Starter €29 = 500, Pro €79 = 1.500, packs 350 €19 · 1.000 €49 · 3.000 €149. Welcome credits 25. Existing balances were converted × 0.3, rounded up (`api/db/20261007143736_credit_repricing.sql`); the app was not live yet. Why: an outbound buyer compares the price per contact, and "10 credits for a phone, 1 per row" did not read as a price. Free searches still cost us vendor calls, so one needs a positive balance and an organization gets at most 1.000 free rows a day (`PER_DAY` in `app/agent/free_rows.py`, counted in Redis, fail-open); past it a search fails with `over_budget`. Alternative left out: rows at 1 credit per 10, which keeps list-only use paid.

**Plan changes (#417, owner, 2026-10-09).** An upgrade takes effect at once and charges the difference between the two plan prices, on any day of the period: Starter to Pro costs €50 + VAT. The renewal date does not change, and the renewal charges the new plan's price. After the upgrade the period has the new plan's allowance minus the plan credits it already used; purchased credits do not change. A downgrade takes effect at the renewal: the period keeps the credits it paid for, and the renewal grants the lower plan's allowance. At every renewal the unused plan credits expire and the full allowance of the plan arrives, so plan credits never pile up. Why: a prorated upgrade let a user upgrade on the last day for about €1.66, take the full difference in credits, and downgrade again (#417). An upgrade that waits for the renewal teaches users to wait before they upgrade, and Zenbree has to pay off on the day they want more. How: `POST /v1/billing/plan` (`app/billing/plan_change.py`) prorates the upgrade from the period start, so Stripe's invoice credits the whole old price and charges the whole new one, and it applies the change only once that invoice is paid (`pending_if_incomplete`). A downgrade is a Stripe subscription schedule. The webhook grants upgrade credits only when the invoice charged at least the price difference. The Stripe customer portal keeps cancellation, cards and invoices; its plan switch is off, because it can only prorate or charge the full new price. Alternative left out: the portal's prorated switch, and a one-off invoice item next to an unprorated switch, which takes two Stripe calls that can half-fail.

## E13: signals and two-way CRM (#119)

- **Stop on reply across campaigns and channels (#931, owner, 2026-10-16).** A human reply stops every sequence for that person in the project: all owners' campaigns, email, LinkedIn and external sequencers (Instantly), on every identity we hold for the person (#937). It also keeps today's reach: the replying owner's campaigns across the organization (`reply_targets.py`). An opt-out or a hard bounce keeps today's scope: exclusions on every identity in every project that wrote to the person (`replies._exclude`). It also persists a stop for each connected sequencer, retries it, and shows the stop as pending until the provider confirms it; new native outreach to the person waits until then. An autoresponder pauses the same people and never stops them. `resume_paused` and the autoresponder path do not bring back a person who replied. Parallel enrollment in two campaigns (for example native LinkedIn and Instantly email) needs the campaign option `block_active_duplicates = false`, exposed in the campaign UI; no new flag. The option never narrows the stop: a reply to either campaign stops both. One system owns a person's email in a project, enforced at the claim. Known open windows: an Instantly outage during a native opt-out (Instantly can send a step that falls due), a lost Instantly event until reconciliation (#930), and a native send already in `sending`. Alternatives left out: a reply that stops only its own campaign or channel, and parallel campaigns allowed by default.
- **Instantly signals: the vote, opt-outs by text and hard bounces (#931, 2026-10-17).** These rules apply the owner's decision above to Instantly facts. An Instantly reply gets two votes: Instantly's (`is_auto_reply`, or the webhook event `auto_reply_received`) and Zenbree's. Zenbree votes only on a marker in the reply's subject or text: an automatic-reply prefix (`is_auto_reply`) or an opt-out (`is_opt_out`). A reply with neither marker gets no vote from Zenbree, so an Instantly autoresponder vote still pauses it. Between the votes cast, the safer class wins: opt-out, then human, then autoresponder. A reply that neither side voted on waits for its API record, which brings Instantly's vote. When the key may not read emails, no API record comes, and the reply counts as human. That default is derived on each read and never stored, so a later vote still decides. Each fact's native effect applies once: a later fact never runs an earlier stop again, so a deleted exclusion or a new enrollment holds. An opt-out that Zenbree reads in the reply's text stops the person's sequences and queues the Instantly stops, as a human reply does, but writes no exclusion. That text has no DMARC verdict, and a native reply opt-out without a DMARC pass also writes none (`replies._may_exclude`). Only Instantly's own unsubscribe (`lead_unsubscribed`, or lead status -2) writes exclusions. A bounce is hard only when Instantly shows the lead at status -1 in a leads pass. The bounce then writes exclusions with today's opt-out scope (origin `bounced`) and stops the person. A webhook bounce alone stops nothing, because the webhook does not say whether a bounce is hard or soft. An Instantly signal acts only inside the organization of the Instantly connection. Known limits: Instantly does not document whether status -1 can also follow a soft bounce, and, while the key may not read emails, an autoresponder that comes only as `reply_received` with no automatic-reply prefix counts as human. Alternatives left out: an exclusion from an opt-out read in the text, and a hard bounce from the webhook event alone.
- **First automations: triggers, actions and approval (#939, owner, 2026-10-16).** An automation starts from a manual run or a schedule; event triggers (signals, HubSpot, segments) come later. Its first actions are: find the people of an account (free), qualify with AI (1 credit a row), find emails (1 credit when found), add people to a campaign, and write contacts and companies to HubSpot. It never sends a message and never activates a campaign. Each automation has a credit cap per run that the user sets, at most 100 (the watchlist `CREDIT_CAP`); a run that reaches it stops and tells the user. Approval: the people of the first run of a new or edited automation enter the campaign for review (`draft`); once the user approves that run, later runs add people with the campaign's normal status (`enrolled_status`), and each message still follows the campaign's `approve_before_sending`. Exclusions, replies and #931's stop rules apply before any credit is spent. Later actions: phone numbers (8 credits), adding exclusions, HubSpot tasks, Instantly (#929), webhooks. Alternatives left out: every run reviewed person by person; a relevance-score threshold that lets strong matches skip review; no cap per automation.
- **Finding people when a signal fires (#941, owner, 2026-10-16).** #648's behavior stays the default: a signal adds only people already in the project's lists and spends nothing (`watchlists/enroll.py`). Finding new people is opt-in for each watchlist. When it is on, an account's signal first checks the account's fit against the project's business profile, apart from the signal's score; with no evidence of fit the account does not qualify, and no buying intent is invented. Then it finds people with the job titles the watchlist names and their emails, reusing known people and contacts first, and bills by the delivered-result contract. Each watchlist has a credit cap per run that the user sets, at most 100. People enter the campaign as in #939: the first run of a new or edited setting is reviewed (`draft`), later runs use the campaign's normal status, and each message follows `approve_before_sending`. It never sends a message or activates a campaign; exclusions, replies and the #931 rules apply before any credit is spent. Alternatives left out: on by default, every run reviewed person by person, no cap per watchlist.
- **Segment exit and first publication (#938, owner, 2026-10-17).** A segment decides who starts, not who continues. A person who leaves a segment stays in every campaign they are in, and the exit only stops new entries and new automation runs for that person. Replies, opt-outs and the #931 stop rules still stop the campaign. The person's history stays. At the first publication, the segment asks whether to include the people who already match. The question shows their count and the most the entry actions can cost. With no answer, only people who join later emit an entry event. Why: HubSpot workflows also keep a person who no longer matches unless the user turns on unenrollment, and users who skip HubSpot's "enroll existing records" question later find that nothing enrolled. Alternatives left out: an exit that stops the unsent steps, and a first publication that processes every current member at once.
- **HubSpot sync conflicts and list removals (#945, owner, 2026-10-17).** HubSpot is the source of truth for a mapped field. Zenbree writes a field only when the HubSpot value is empty, and never overwrites a value that HubSpot holds. The sync runs once a day and on a manual refresh. A contact who leaves the source HubSpot list stays in the Zenbree list, marked as left the source. The row keeps its history and opt-outs, and no new automation starts for it. Why: one direction for each value cannot form a write loop, and it matches the list merge rule (existing values win). Clay has no built-in fill-empty-only option, so its users build workarounds. Clay also keeps rows that leave a HubSpot list and refreshes once a day. Alternatives left out: an owner for each field, the newest value wins, and a row deleted when it leaves the list.
- **Outcome attribution (#942, owner, 2026-10-17).** A positive reply, a meeting or a deal counts once. It goes to the campaign that received the reply, and the report shows the list, the source and the signal the person came from. Reports count by the date of the event. A meeting or a deal counts only when HubSpot holds one for that contact, through the contact's association. An interested label is neither. Why: one conversion with one owner cannot be counted twice, and its lineage still answers which source works. Instantly also gives an opportunity to the campaign that got the positive reply. HubSpot keeps multi-touch models for Enterprise plans. Alternative left out: first-touch and last-touch models with a switch.
- **Mailbox warm-up and provisioning (#936, owner, 2026-10-17).** Zenbree builds no native warm-up and buys no domains or mailboxes. A user who wants warm-up uses Instantly (#929), and Zenbree shows those mailboxes as managed by Instantly. The current ramp of daily limits stays, and the UI never calls the ramp warm-up. Why: Instantly and Smartlead include warm-up in every plan, and independent tests found that warm-up did not raise replies. SPF, DKIM, DMARC and list checks raise them more, and Zenbree already checks DNS. Alternative left out: a warm-up provider integrated in Zenbree.
- **Signal hint on proposed segments (#191, owner, 2026-10-17).** A proposed onboarding segment can carry a signal to look for, such as open job postings. The LLM names the signal from the business profile, and the card labels it as a hint ("Look for: open job postings"), never as evidence. It costs no credits. A checked signal comes from watchlists (#122). Alternatives left out: a real check during onboarding, which costs credits, and no hint at all.
- **Inbound webhook trigger (#1031, owner, 2026-10-17).** Each signed call to an automation's webhook carries one person. Zenbree finds the person by email or LinkedIn URL and starts one run for that person. A call that repeats an earlier one, by its idempotency key, starts nothing new. A call with neither identifier fails with an error code and starts nothing. Why: one person for each call keeps validation, billing and the per-run credit cap the same as a manual run. Clay webhook tables also make one row for each call, and HubSpot webhook triggers enroll one record for each call, matched by a property. Alternatives left out: a list of people in one call, and a bare run-now call with no person.
- **Signal trigger (#1030, owner, 2026-10-21).** An automation can start from the signals of a watchlist of its project. Three owner decisions:
  1. The score rule is #648's (`watchlists/enroll.py`): an account is ready when the live scores (`signal_score`) of its signals that the automation has not used yet add up to the minimum the user sets. Each automation uses a signal once, apart from #648 and from every other automation.
  2. A run works on the account of its signals. A `find_people` step looks at that account only, in the watchlist's company list, and moves no rotation. Then the run takes the people of its list who work at the account, matched as #648 matches them, the best first.
  3. Only signals first seen after the trigger names its watchlist start runs, so the first publication does not start a run for each old signal. Changing the watchlist starts that count again. Changing the minimum or the definition does not.

  Two defaults of this PR, not owner decisions, open to change: a resume also starts the count again, so the signals that came while the automation was paused start nothing, as webhook calls are refused while it is paused. And a run uses its account's signals when it takes its people, or when it succeeds with only a find-people step. A run that failed before that gives them back, and its account waits 15 minutes before its next run while the other accounts go on. After 3 failed runs in a row the account's signals count as used, and only new signals start it again. A run cancelled by a new version gives them back for the next beat. A pause, a watchlist change or a switch away from the signal trigger starts the count again, so the signals of a run cancelled that way never count again. A run the user cancelled used them. Unlike #648, which keeps the signals of an account with no known person, a run that takes nobody because nobody is at the account used them. The run's summary says why it took nobody (`account_gone`, `no_new_person`, `all_refused`). Alternatives left out: a run over the whole list for each signal, and old signals that start runs at the first publication.
- **HubSpot field write-back (#924, owner, 2026-10-17).** The rule of #945 applies: Zenbree fills only HubSpot fields that are empty and never replaces a value that HubSpot holds. The first objects are contacts and companies. The first property types are text, number and dropdown. For each run, the user picks the fields to write and confirms a preview first. The post-send write-back keeps its current contract. Alternative left out: an option to overwrite fields that already have a value.
- **Several mailbox accounts per client, rotated per campaign (#935, owner, 2026-10-17).** A client links any number of Google and Microsoft accounts, and each campaign picks the accounts it sends from by id, 20 at most (`outreach_campaign_senders`). A new campaign starts with the client's connected accounts, and the user can remove them. A campaign with an email step and no account that can send does not activate (`CAMPAIGN.NO_SENDER`). Each conversation gets its account when its first email is claimed and keeps it for every step, retry and restart (`outreach_campaign_targets.sender_id`). Cap, ramp, pacing and send window belong to the account and count every client and campaign that uses it. A conversation whose account cannot send is held with a reason, and nothing moves it to another account: an account the client unlinked holds its conversations until a user links it back. An uncertain send stays held, and no action marks it sent or failed. The provider-name pool (`outreach_campaigns.sender_pool`, `outreach_campaign_targets.sender_provider`) is removed. Why: a pool of provider names resolved to whichever account the client linked at send time, so replacing an account moved live threads to a new mailbox and broke their Gmail threads. Instantly keeps its own accounts and limits outside this model. Alternatives left out: one account per provider, and moving a held conversation to another account.

## Billing

- **Plan-credit expiry and open holds (#962).** Credits in an open hold at a renewal are not spent yet, and spending uses the allowance first, so they expire first: the renewal records them per hold and takes them when the hold returns credits. What a hold spent of its renewal amount counts as old allowance. So a run that spends 10 credits of a hold open across a renewal leaves the new period whole (750), while 10 spent after the renewal come out of the new allowance (740).
